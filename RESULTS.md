# The best and worst Premier League signings since 2016, explained simply

**The question:** which big-money signings (€20m, roughly £17m, or more) actually made their clubs better on the pitch? We left the price tag out entirely and judged every player only by what he did once he arrived.

## The score in one sentence

**Points above replacement** = how many extra league points a player won for his club, compared with a cheap, ordinary squad player the club could have used instead.

If a player scores **+10**, his club finished with roughly 10 more league points over his time there than it would have with a run-of-the-mill backup in his position. Ten points is about three extra wins.

Football fans know this idea from baseball, where it's called WAR (wins above replacement).

## The top 10 signings

| Rank | Player | Club | Points above replacement | In plain words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mohamed Salah | Liverpool | +35 | About 4 extra points every season for eight seasons, and his best year was his most recent (2024-25). |
| 2 | Virgil van Dijk | Liverpool | +31 | His first full season (2018-19) alone was worth about 8 points. |
| 3 | Rodri | Man City | +28 | About 5 points a season as the midfielder who makes City tick. |
| 4 | Ilkay Gündoğan | Man City | +25 | Seven seasons of steady value, peaking in 2020-21. |
| 5 | Alisson | Liverpool | +24 | Above a backup goalkeeper in six of his seven seasons. |
| 6 | Bruno Fernandes | Man United | +21 | Above replacement in every season since arriving in January 2020. |
| 7 | Erling Haaland | Man City | +21 | Only three seasons, but the best per-season figure in the top 10: almost 7 points a year. |
| 8 | Marcos Alonso | Chelsea | +21 | Became a title-winning wing-back in his first season. |
| 9 | Kyle Walker | Man City | +20 | Eight seasons of reliable right-back play. |
| 10 | N'Golo Kanté | Chelsea | +19 | Won the league in his first Chelsea season and kept delivering. |

**Why longevity matters:** a player who is good for eight years helps his club more than one who is brilliant for one. That is why Haaland, despite the best rate of anyone, sits seventh. Give him a few more seasons and he will climb.

**A note on "one-and-done" signings:** if a club sells or loans out a player after just one season, he loses about 1.5 points. That's the value of the season he didn't give the club. Players whose club was relegated are not penalised for leaving.

## Who actually improved on the player before them?

Since 2019 we also compared each signing with the player who held that position before. That player was judged on his average over the previous three seasons.

- **Biggest upgrades:** Alexander Isak over Chris Wood at Newcastle (+4.4 points a season), Erling Haaland over Gabriel Jesus at City (+3.9), Bruno Guimarães at Newcastle (+3.9).
- **Biggest step down:** Daniel James at Leeds, compared with Raphinha (-5.4 points a season).
- **The surprising bit:** only about a third of signings were clearly better than the player already there. Clubs often sell a player right after his best season, which leaves a high bar to clear.

## How sure are we?

We reran the whole ranking 1,000 times, each time shuffling which matches counted, to see how much luck moves the results.

- **Rock solid:** Salah, Van Dijk and Rodri are top 3 to 6 in every rerun. Daniel James is last in every rerun.
- **Too close to call:** places 5 to 15 swap around a lot. Kanté, 10th, makes the top 10 in only about half the reruns.
- **What this can't tell you:** the reruns only measure luck. They don't fix the blind spots of the stats themselves, such as undervaluing tacklers.

## What this ranking does not do

- It doesn't use transfer fees, so it doesn't say who was good *value*.
- It only covers Premier League seasons from 2016-17 to 2024-25, and signings up to January 2023.
- It can't fully separate a player from his manager or teammates. When a whole team improves, every regular shares the credit.
